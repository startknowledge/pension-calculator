Assets Test: Should the Family Home be Included in the Age Pension Calculation?

The age pension is a vital source of income for many retirees in Australia. However, the assets test, which is used to determine eligibility for the age pension, has been a topic of debate in recent years. According to the Australian Institute of Health and Welfare (2019), the age pension is the main source of income for approximately 60% of retirees. Introduction to the Assets Test

The assets test is a means test used to determine eligibility for the age pension. It assesses the value of a person's assets, including cash, investments, and property, to determine their eligibility for the age pension. The test is designed to ensure that those who have the means to support themselves do not receive the age pension. However, the test has been criticized for being overly complex and unfair, particularly with regards to the treatment of the family home.

According to the Australian Bureau of Statistics (2020), the majority of retirees own their own home, with approximately 80% of retirees being homeowners. The family home is often the largest asset owned by retirees, and its exclusion from the assets test has been a topic of debate. Some argue that the family home should be included in the assets test, as it is a significant asset that could be used to support oneself in retirement. Others argue that the family home is a essential part of retirement security and should be exempt from the assets test.

Should the Family Home be Included in the Age Pension Calculation?

There are valid arguments both for and against including the family home in the age pension calculation. On the one hand, including the family home in the assets test could help to ensure that the age pension is targeted towards those who need it most. It could also help to reduce the burden on the taxpayer, as those who have significant assets, such as a family home, would be less likely to receive the age pension.

On the other hand, excluding the family home from the assets test could help to ensure that retirees have a sense of security and stability in retirement. The family home is often a vital part of retirement planning, providing a place to live and a sense of community. Including the family home in the assets test could lead to retirees feeling forced to sell their home in order to access the age pension, which could have significant social and emotional implications.
